NōtoNawa Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 15, 2026

The short version

  • NōtoNawa is a private journal for rope practice. Everything you put in your journal is encrypted on your device before it leaves it: your ties, plans, workshops, people, locations, tags, notes, photos, videos, drawings and poses. We can't read any of it, and neither can anyone who gets hold of our servers.
  • You sign in with a passkey. There are no passwords, and we don't ask for your name. A recovery email is optional, and we keep only a scrambled copy we can't read.
  • Keep a second passkey and your recovery code. If you lose them all, your journal can't be recovered, by you or by us.
  • No ads, no tracking, no third-party analytics, and we never sell or share your data. We only count basic totals, such as how many accounts are active (see Usage statistics).
  • You can share things with other people, by link or through a connection between your accounts. What you share is encrypted too, and our server keeps no record of who is connected to whom.
  • You can export everything, or delete your account, at any time.

Who we are

NōtoNawa is run by Juice aka Mx_Piggie ("we", "us") in Seattle, WA, USA. Contact us about privacy, give feedback, or tell us how frickin cool we are at contact@notonawa.app.

What we store

Your journal, encrypted

Each entry in your journal (a tie, plan, workshop, person, location, tag, drawing, pose, preset, your settings for role names and the poser's body, and each connection) is encrypted on your device and stored as a sealed record. Photos and videos are encrypted on your device and stored as sealed files.

All our server can see of them is:

  • what kind each record is (for example "tie" or "person"), so it can tell how many of each you have;
  • roughly how big each one is (records are padded, so their size says little), which counts toward your 1 GB;
  • the order your changes were made in, so your other devices can catch up;
  • for each photo or video, that it's a photo or a video, its size and when it was uploaded. Nothing on our server says which entry it belongs to.

When you delete an entry, we keep a small marker (its random id and its kind, nothing of what it was) so your other devices know to remove it too.

Your account

  • Your passkeys: the public part only (your device keeps the secret part), with the name you gave each one and when it was added and last used.
  • Your sessions: a scrambled copy of each sign-in's token, which passkey was used, and when it started and was last used. We don't store your IP address or a description of your device.
  • Your recovery code, scrambled. The code is made on your device and we never receive it, only a scrambled value made from it.
  • Your recovery email, scrambled, if you add one. Your device scrambles it before sending it, so we can't read it or email you. We use it only to check that someone asking us to recover an account is its owner.
  • Your encryption key, locked, so we can't use it (see How encryption works), and a public key other accounts use to connect with you.
  • A few settings: light or dark mode, the last release notes you saw, and whether you've hidden the welcome. They're readable by our server.
  • Your agreement to this policy, which version, and when; and that you joined with an invite (not who it was from).
  • Messages from us you've dismissed, and any meant only for you.
  • Bug reports and ideas you send us, as you wrote them. These aren't encrypted (we need to read them), so please leave journal details out.

Technical information

Our hosting provider, Cloudflare, briefly handles standard connection information such as your IP address to deliver the app, and counts requests per IP address for a minute at a time to stop abuse. We don't store it and we keep no request logs.

Usage statistics

To understand how NōtoNawa is used and which features matter, we count a few things from what we already store: how many accounts there are, how many have been used recently (from when their passkeys and sessions were last used), how many entries of each kind accounts have (for example ties, plans or poses), and how much storage they use. We look at these as totals and spreads across accounts, not at what any one person is doing, and we never combine them with information from outside NōtoNawa. We can't see what's in any entry, only its kind and size.

How encryption works

  • When you create your account, your device makes a random encryption key. Every entry gets a key of its own, locked with yours, and is encrypted with it (AES-256-GCM) on your device.
  • Your key is locked with a secret your passkey produces, which never leaves your device, and with your recovery code. We store only these locked copies.
  • If you lose every passkey and your recovery code, your journal can't be recovered, by you or by us. That's the cost of us not being able to read it. Keep a second passkey (on another device, or a hardware security key) and keep your recovery code somewhere safe.

On your device

  • Nothing decrypted is stored on your device. Your device keeps a copy of your journal still sealed, so the app opens quickly, and decrypts it in memory only while the app is unlocked. Signing out, or deleting your account, removes that copy.
  • Searching and sorting happen on your device. What you search for is never sent to us.
  • The app locks itself after 15 minutes without use, after 5 minutes in the background, and when you close or reload it, forgetting your key and everything it decrypted.
  • Decryption happens in a walled-off part of the browser, and only our own code can run in the app.

Your operating system may copy memory to disk, and browser extensions you allow on this site can see what's on screen, so use full-disk encryption and be careful which extensions you allow.

Photos and videos

  • Before a photo or video leaves your device, the app rebuilds it as a new, smaller file, so none of its hidden details (GPS location, dates, the camera or phone it was made with) are kept. Your original is never uploaded.
  • Each account can store up to 1 GB in all, with up to 30 photos and videos (5 of them videos) on each tie, plan or workshop.
  • We never scan or analyse your photos or videos.
  • Only store photos and videos of people who have agreed to it.

Sharing with other people

By link

  • You can share your profile, or a copy of a tie, plan or workshop, as a link or QR code. You choose exactly what goes in it.
  • It's encrypted on your device with a key that's only in the link, after the "#", which browsers never send to us. We store the encrypted copy and can't open it, so send the link only to the person it's for.
  • We store the share's kind, its size, when it ends, how many times it can be and has been opened (not by whom), and its name (encrypted). It counts toward your 1 GB until it ends.
  • It's deleted when it ends, when its last allowed use is opened, or as soon as you revoke it. Revoking doesn't take back what someone already added.

Connecting accounts

  • A profile invite can offer to connect your accounts, if you tick it and the person opening it agrees.
  • Our server keeps no record that two accounts are connected. Each of you keeps the connection in your own encrypted journal. Your devices pass each other messages (profile updates, and things you send) through mailboxes on our server: a mailbox has an owner but no record of who writes to it, and every message is encrypted so only the two of you can read it. Messages are deleted once fetched, or after 90 days.
  • Through a connection you can share your profile (it updates when you change it, with the same fields), and send a tie, plan or workshop, which is stored as a one-use share that ends after 30 days.
  • You can each check a connection's safety number in person, to be sure no one is in between.
  • Either of you can remove a connection at any time. It ends for both of you, and you can also clear the About you shared from their card.

Information about other people

Your journal may hold information about the people you tie with. Please only record what they would be comfortable with, and remove it if they ask. If they also use NōtoNawa, they can't see your entries, and we don't tell them you've written about them. When you share a tie, plan or workshop, share only what the people on it would be comfortable with.

Where your data is stored

NōtoNawa runs on Cloudflare (Workers, D1 database and R2 storage), our only service provider with access to stored data. We ask Cloudflare to store photos and videos in North America [and the database in enam]; its network may handle data in other countries. Everything is also encrypted at rest by Cloudflare.

When you paste a short Google Maps link (maps.app.goo.gl) into a location, our server asks Google where it points, to fill in its coordinates. Google sees our server, not you, and only the link. Full Google Maps links are read on your device.

Cookies and browser storage

We use one cookie, to keep you signed in. It can't be read by scripts, is sent only to NōtoNawa over secure connections, and expires after 30 days (7 without use). Your browser also keeps the app's own code, the sealed copy of your journal (see On your device), and the word "light" or "dark" so pages show the right colors before you sign in. Nothing is used for tracking.

How long we keep your data

  • What you store is kept until you delete it. Deleting removes it from our live database and storage straight away.
  • Shares and messages are deleted when they end, as above.
  • When you delete your account, everything in it is deleted straight away.
  • Backups. Our database provider keeps a rolling history for disaster recovery, and we back up the database every day, encrypted with a key that only Mx_Piggie holds, away from our servers. Both are gone after 30 days. Photos and videos aren't backed up. We restore only to recover from a failure, never to bring back something you deleted unless you ask.
  • When your account was updated to full encryption, our backups and our database provider's history still held the older, partly readable version of your journal; that copy is gone 30 days after the update.
  • Sessions end when you sign out, and expired ones are cleared daily.

Your rights and choices

Wherever you live, you can:

  • Export everything, decrypted on your device into a file only you hold (with your photos and videos, as a .zip). The file isn't encrypted, so keep it somewhere safe.
  • Import entries from an export or a spreadsheet. The file is read and encrypted on your device.
  • Correct or delete anything in the app, or delete your whole account.
  • End sessions or remove passkeys from your account settings.
  • Contact us at contact@notonawa.app. We will respond within 10 days.

Depending on where you live (for example the EU, UK or California), you may have further rights under laws such as the GDPR or CCPA, including the right to complain to your data protection authority.

Legal basis (EU/UK users). We store your journal because you ask us to in order to provide the service. Information about your or other people's sex lives is special-category data, which we process only with your explicit consent, given when you create your account. You can withdraw it at any time by deleting your data or your account. We count usage statistics because we have a legitimate interest in improving the service; they're totals, worked out from data we already hold for running your account.

Our own access

The only person who can access NōtoNawa's systems is Mx_Piggie, using an account protected by a hardware security key. We don't browse accounts, and we can't read your journal under any circumstances.

We can't restore your journal if you lose every passkey and your recovery code. If you added a recovery email, we can help you sign in again: contact us from that address, we'll send a one-time code to it, and when you send it back we'll give your account a new recovery code. That restores signing in only. Your journal still needs one of your own passkeys or your earlier recovery code; without either, you can start again, but the old journal can't be read.

Legal requests

If we receive a legally valid demand for data, we can only provide what we store (see What we store), and we can't decrypt your journal. Unless the law forbids it, we will tell you in the app before handing anything over.

Security incidents

If a breach affects your data, we will tell you in the app as quickly as we can, and notify regulators where the law requires.

What we don't do

  • sell, rent or share your data
  • show ads or use your data for advertising
  • track how you use the app: no analytics or session-recording tools, tracking pixels, third-party scripts or request logs (beyond the totals in Usage statistics)
  • train AI models on your data or send it to AI services
  • let anyone else see your journal, except what you choose to share

Messages from us

We can show you messages in the app, for example about a change to this policy or a problem with the service. They're written by us as plain text. We record which ones you've dismissed, so they don't come back.

Changes to this policy

If we change this policy, we will update the date at the top. If a change would reduce your privacy, we will ask for your agreement in the app first. Until you agree, nothing new can be saved to your journal, but you can still export your data or delete your account.
