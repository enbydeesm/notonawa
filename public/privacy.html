The short version

  • Rope Journal is a private journal for rope practice. It is built so that the most sensitive parts, your notes and your photos, are encrypted on your device before they leave it. We can't read them, and neither can anyone who gets hold of our servers.
  • You sign in with a passkey. There are no passwords, and we don't ask for your legal name or a sign-in through Google, Apple or anyone else. An email address is optional, only for account recovery, and we keep only a scrambled copy that we can't read.
  • Keep a second passkey and your recovery code. If you lose them all, we can't guarantee to get you back in, and your encrypted notes and photos can't be recovered by anyone.
  • No ads, no analytics, no tracking, no selling or sharing your data. The app loads no scripts or fonts from other companies.
  • Some information has to stay readable by our server so the app can sort and filter it, such as names, titles, dates and ratings. The full list is in What we can and can't read.
  • You can export everything, import entries from a file, or delete your account and everything in it, at any time.

What we collect

Your account

  • A passkey. When you create a passkey, your device keeps the secret part. We store only the public part, an ID for it, the name you give it (e.g. "My phone") and when it was created and last used. A passkey can't be used to identify you outside Rope Journal.
  • Your sessions. When you sign in we give your browser a random session token in a cookie. We store a scrambled (hashed) copy of it, which passkey you signed in with, when the session started, and when it was last used (to the hour), so you can see and end your sessions. We don't store your IP address or a description of your device.
  • Your recovery code, scrambled. The code is made on your device and we never receive it. We store only a scrambled (hashed) value derived from it, which can't be turned back into the code.
  • Your recovery email, scrambled, if you add one. It's optional. Your device scrambles the address (a slow, salted hash) before sending it, and we store only that, so we can't read the address or send you email. We use it for one thing: checking that someone asking us to recover an account is its owner (see Our own access).
  • Your agreement. When you confirmed you're 18 or older and agreed to this policy, and which version of it you agreed to.
  • Your invite. Accounts are by invite for now. We record that an invite was used, and by which account, but nothing about who it was for.
  • Your encryption keys, locked. Your notes and photos are encrypted with a key that only exists unlocked on your own device. We store copies of it that are themselves locked by your passkeys and by your recovery code, so we can't use them. See How encryption works.

We don't collect your phone number, real name, contacts or location, or your email address unless you add a recovery email.

Your journal

What you choose to write in the app: ties, plans, people, locations, tags, and (once photos are available) photos. Some of this is encrypted so only you can read it, and some isn't (see the next section).

Technical information

To deliver the app and protect it from attacks, our hosting provider, Cloudflare, briefly processes standard connection information such as your IP address, and counts sign-in attempts per IP address for a minute at a time to stop people guessing. We don't store it, and we don't keep request logs. We don't use analytics, advertising or tracking tools of any kind.

What we can and can't read

Encrypted on your device. Only you can read it:

  • notes on ties, and each participant's notes on a tie
  • notes on people, and their profile notes (injuries, modifications, likes, dislikes and limits)
  • notes on locations
  • plan descriptions and notes
  • photos, their thumbnails, and any captions

Readable by our server, so the app can list, sort and filter them:

  • tie titles, dates, types, durations and ratings; who took part and in what role; which plans and location were used
  • people's names, pronouns and usual roles
  • location names, map links and coordinates
  • plan titles, stages, difficulty and versions
  • tags
  • when things were created, changed or archived, and how they link together

This second list is still private to your account: the app never shows it to anyone else, and we don't look at it (see Our own access). But because it isn't encrypted on your device, it could be disclosed if our systems were breached or if we were legally required to hand it over. Keep anything you consider truly sensitive, such as full names, addresses or identifying details, in the notes fields, which are encrypted. We plan to encrypt more of these fields over time.

How encryption works

  • When you create your account, your device makes a random encryption key. Your notes and photos are encrypted with it (AES-256-GCM) on your device, before anything is sent to us.
  • That key is locked using a secret your passkey produces (the WebAuthn "PRF" feature), which never leaves your device. If your passkey doesn't support this, you unlock with your recovery code instead.
  • You get a recovery code when you create your account. It can also unlock your key. We show it once and don't keep a copy.
  • If you lose every passkey and your recovery code, your encrypted notes and photos can't be recovered, by you or by us. That is the cost of us not being able to read them. We strongly recommend keeping a second passkey (on another device, or a hardware security key) and keeping your recovery code somewhere safe, such as a password manager or on paper.

How the app protects your data on your device

  • Nothing decrypted is saved on your device. Your notes are decrypted in your browser's memory only while the app is open and unlocked, and are never written to the browser's storage, its cache or its form history. Our pages and data are sent with instructions not to be cached. The browser's spellcheck is turned off in the app, because some browsers can send the text being checked to their maker. The one exception is an export, which saves a file wherever you choose (see Your rights and choices).
  • The app locks itself. It forgets your key and everything it decrypted after [15] minutes without use, after [5] minutes in the background, when you sign out, and when you close the tab. You unlock again with your passkey.
  • Decryption happens in a separate, walled-off part of the browser (a Web Worker), so the rest of the page only ever sees the results it is displaying.
  • Searching happens on your device. What you type in the search box is never sent to us and never appears in the page address, so it doesn't end up in your browser history either.
  • Only our own code runs in the app. Strict browser security rules (Content Security Policy and Trusted Types) block scripts from anywhere else, and the app can't be embedded in other websites.

Browsers have limits that no app can fully overcome. Your operating system may copy memory to disk (for example when your computer sleeps or hibernates), and browser extensions you install can see what's on the page. Using full-disk encryption on your devices, and being careful which extensions you allow on this site, protects you further.

Photos

  • Before a photo leaves your device, the app removes its hidden metadata, including GPS location, camera details and timestamps.
  • The photo and its thumbnail are then encrypted on your device and stored in a private storage area that isn't reachable from the internet. Photos are only ever handed back to your own signed-in account, and only your device can decrypt them.
  • We never scan, analyse, tag or share your photos, and no other company processes them.
  • Only store photos of people who have agreed to it. You are responsible for having their consent.

Information about other people

Your journal may hold information about the people you tie with. Please only record what they would be comfortable with, and remove it if they ask. Entries about someone stay in your account. If that person also uses Rope Journal, they can't see your entries, and we don't tell them you've written about them.

Where your data is stored

Rope Journal runs on Cloudflare (Workers, D1 database and R2 storage), which is our only service provider with access to stored data. Data is stored [in REGION / "in Cloudflare's network, which may include countries other than your own"] and is encrypted at rest by Cloudflare, in addition to our own encryption of your notes and photos.

Cookies

We use one cookie: your session cookie. It's needed to keep you signed in. It can't be read by scripts, is only sent over secure connections and only to Rope Journal, and expires after [30] days, or sooner after [7] days without use. We use no other cookies, local storage for tracking, or similar technologies. If you switch between light and dark mode, the app remembers that choice in your browser's local storage (just the word "light" or "dark"); it never leaves your device.

How long we keep your data

  • While you have an account, we keep what you've stored until you delete it.
  • When you delete something, it's removed from our live database straight away. Deleted photos are removed from storage straight away.
  • When you delete your account, everything in it (journal, photos, passkeys, sessions, recovery email and locked keys) is deleted straight away. Your device or password manager may still list your passkey for Rope Journal; where the browser supports it, the app tells it the passkey no longer works.
  • Backups. Our database provider keeps a rolling history for disaster recovery, so deleted data can remain there for up to 7 days before it disappears permanently. Our own backups are encrypted and kept for no more than 7 days. We would only restore from these to recover from a failure, never to bring back something you deleted unless you ask us to.
  • Signed-out sessions are deleted when you sign out, and expired sessions are cleared every day.

Your rights and choices

Wherever you live, you can:

  • Export all your data from the app at any time, including your notes and photos, decrypted on your device into a file only you hold. It contains your whole journal, plus when you created your account and agreed to this policy, your passkeys' names and dates, and whether you've set a recovery email (not the address, which we don't have). The file isn't encrypted, so keep it somewhere safe.
  • Import entries from an export, or ties from a spreadsheet (a CSV file). The file is read in your browser, and only the entries in it are sent to us, just as if you'd typed them in.
  • Correct anything by editing it in the app.
  • Delete individual entries, or your whole account, from the app.
  • End sessions on any device, or remove passkeys, from your account settings.
  • Contact us at [CONTACT EMAIL] with any privacy question or request. We will respond within [30] days.

Depending on where you live (for example the EU, UK or California), you may have further rights under laws such as the GDPR or CCPA, including the right to complain to your data protection authority.

Our own access

The only person who can access Rope Journal's systems is [OPERATOR NAME], using an account protected by a hardware security key. We don't browse users' data. We would look at the readable parts of an account only when you ask us to (for example to help with a problem), or when the law requires it. Encrypted notes and photos can't be read by us under any circumstances.

Keep a second passkey and your recovery code: we can't guarantee that we can restore your account or your data if you lose them all. We may be able to help you sign in again, but only if you added a recovery email:

  • Contact us from that address. We'll send a one-time code to it from our own mailbox, and you send it back, so we know the address is yours (an email that merely appears to come from an address doesn't prove it).
  • We then give your account a new recovery code. We only do this when you ask.
  • Without a recovery email we have no way to tell that an account is yours, so we can't help.

A new recovery code restores signing in and the parts of your journal we can read. It never brings back your encrypted notes and photos, which still need one of your own passkeys or your own earlier recovery code.

Legal requests

If we receive a legally valid demand for data, we can only provide what we can read (see What we can and can't read). We cannot decrypt your notes or photos. Unless the law forbids it, we will tell you about the request before handing anything over.

Security incidents

If a breach affects your data, we will tell you through the app as quickly as we can (we can't email you: we only hold a scrambled copy of any recovery email), and notify regulators where the law requires.
